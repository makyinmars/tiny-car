THE SHORT VERSION

A little road etiquette.

You have 90 seconds. Pass traffic without touching it to earn points. Crashes slow you down and reset your clean-driving multiplier, but you keep racing until time runs out.

← → / A D
Steer gently. The car turns more at speed and settles when released.
↑ / W
Hold to accelerate. Release to settle into a gentle cruise.
↓ / S
Hold to slow down or stop. Braking takes priority over gas.
SPACE / ESC
Play, pause, or resume.
On your phone
Hold the steering and gas buttons together.
− / + / 0
Zoom out, zoom in, or reset. Use the mouse wheel over the road or the camera buttons on touch screens.

Brake before tight corners and chicanes. Higher speeds push your car toward the outside curb; steer into the bend and use the width of a clear lane. The route preview and speed guide help you plan ahead. Short straights give you room to accelerate again. Zoom changes your view only.

Traffic follows the three marked lanes and slows for cars ahead. Amber signals warn before lane changes; leave space for merging cars. The gravel shoulders slow you down. A stopped car cannot steer sideways.

Amber crossing markers warn of people preparing to dash or hop from the roadside. The amber line shows how far they may enter. Use shoulders for a brief escape, then return to a clear lane. Give pedestrians room; contact causes the same slowdown and streak reset as a car crash.

Cyan arrows and a rising engine note announce an open-wheel car from behind. Hold a steady line, watch its turn signals, and let it pass. These fast cars yield when blocked and do not award points. Traffic builds in waves, with short quieter stretches to catch your breath.

Each clean pass earns 50 points, plus 30 if you pass within 12 pixels without contact. Passing at 60–79 mph adds 20; at 80+ mph it adds 40. Every five clean passes increases the multiplier for the next pass, up to 5×.

Your challenge uses the same seed and rules on every device. Weekly boards reset Mondays at 00:00 UTC. Tied scores share a rank. Nicknames identify you here; they are not accounts.
